# Elmbridge Library — Archive Digitization

Elmbridge Library is a fictional municipal archive holding newspapers, maps and handwritten letters. The digitization team scans fragile newspapers at 300 dots per inch and uses optical character recognition (OCR) to produce searchable text. Staff review the OCR output because faded ink and unusual typefaces often introduce transcription mistakes. Public access copies are stored as PDF/A.

The library's catalog assigns a persistent identifier to each item and records the date, author and collection name. Conservation staff prefer noncontact imaging for damaged volumes. Original manuscripts remain in climate-controlled storage; the public website offers only derivatives and approved metadata.

The team runs an accessibility review before releasing batches of documents. This workflow is not the museum's ultraviolet-light preservation program and does not include freight schedules or hospital patient records. The annual audit checks whether every digital item can be traced to a physical source.
